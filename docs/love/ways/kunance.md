# Kunance — Preparing-Place-Love

## The gap

The deepest unsung love-register, because its work leaves no spectacle. The room is *just ready* when you arrive. Modern English has *preparation* (utility), *consideration* (etiquette), *welcome* (the moment of arrival). None name the months-of-quiet-architectural-work that precedes the arrival.

Hebrew *kûn* carries the foundational sense — establishing, making-stand-firm, setting-in-place. *Mekon shibtekha* — *"the established place of thy throne"* (Exodus 15:17). The throne is not the establishing; the establishing makes the throne possible. Different verb. Different work.

## The cross-tradition roots

Hebrew *kûn* — establish-as-foundation. Greek *hetoimazō* — make-ready, prepare (the verb of John 14:2: *"I go to prepare a place for you"*). Akkadian *kânu* (cognate of kûn; establish, make-firm, set-up-the-foundation). Sanskrit *sthā-* (cause to stand; foundational-establish). Arabic *aʿadda* (prepare, make-ready). Egyptian *grg* (found, establish, set-up).

The convergence: ancient traditions had a love-verb specific to *making-the-foundation-ready-for-the-other*. Modern English flattened this into utility-language.

## Negative-space evidence

> "He had been making the place ready for years before I knew there was a place."
> "She built the structure that would hold him long before he needed it to hold."
> "The architecture was an act of love, but no one called it that."

The architecture is *the act* — but English has no compact verb that says so.
