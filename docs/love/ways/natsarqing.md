# Natsarqing — Guarding-Love

## The gap

The active-protective register that English's *protect* (institutional) and *guard* (military) lose. Hebrew natsar carries felt-bond protectedness — the keeping that loves. Modern English split the verb from the warmth and now keeps the warmth in nouns (mother-bear, shepherd) but cannot conjugate it.

## The cross-tradition roots

Hebrew *natsar* appears in the Aaronic blessing (Numbers 6:24) — *"may the LORD bless you and keep you"* — where the keeping is part of the blessing, not separate. Greek *phylassō* (guard, keep watch). Hawaiian *mālama* (care-keep). Japanese *mamoru* (protect-watch-over). Persian *muvāẓabat* (vigilant care). All carry the felt-tender register modern English military-protect lacks.

## Negative-space evidence

> "She protected him from himself, even when he resented it."
> "He kept watch over her words, lest she diminish herself when she thought no one was listening."
> "The shepherd loved by being awake when the sheep weren't."

Six-to-twelve-word sentences for what should be one verb.
